What is Tolerance Stack-Up?

Tolerance Stack-Up is a 1D linear analysis method that sums independent dimensional contributors to predict whether an assembly gap or interference stays within specification. In engineering practice, it is used in mechanical design to validate critical functional dimensions such as bearing preload, seal compression, valve lash, and shaft end float before cutting tooling.

PropertyValue
StandardASME Y14.5, ISO 286-1
FormulaX_nom = sum(n_i) ; T_WC = sum(t_i) ; T_RSS = sqrt(sum(t_i^2))

Used InAutomotive, Aerospace, Medical Devices, Precision Machining

Why it matters

Stack-up predicts whether an assembly gap closes before you cut tooling.

Formula relationship

WC sums extremes; RSS uses √(Σt²); Monte Carlo samples assigned distributions.

Typical mistake

Treating every drawing ± block as uniform when the process is centered and capable.

How Tolerance Stack-Up Works

Each contributor is entered as a nominal value, a tolerance band, and a sign. The stack predicts the resulting gap, interference, or overall length before tooling is cut.

Three accumulation philosophies are common: worst-case (guaranteed but conservative), RSS (statistical shortcut for capable processes), and Monte Carlo (empirical simulation for mixed distributions).

Key Variables

  • Nominal (n_i): Centered dimension of each contributor in the chain.
  • Tolerance (t_i): Plus/minus or bilateral band assigned to that contributor.
  • Sign (+/−): Whether the contributor adds to or subtracts from the closed dimension.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating every contributor as independent when machining and fixtures share common datums — this understates correlated variation and can hide scrap risk.
  • Using worst-case alone for high-volume production — it drives unnecessary cost when process capability supports RSS or Monte Carlo.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Tolerance Stack-Up and RSS Tolerance?

Tolerance stack-up is the overall method (worst-case, RSS, or Monte Carlo). RSS is one statistical accumulation option inside that method.

When should I use Tolerance Stack-Up instead of Monte Carlo Simulation?

Use worst-case or RSS first for quick screening. Switch to Monte Carlo when distributions differ, tails matter, or you need predicted PPM/Cpk.
