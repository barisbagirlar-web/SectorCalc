What is Chip Thinning?

Chip Thinning is the reduction in average chip thickness when radial engagement is less than half the tool diameter, requiring increased feed to maintain productivity. In engineering practice, it is used to optimize high-speed machining strategies and prevent excessive tool wear from thin chips.

PropertyValue
StandardTool manufacturer guidelines
Formulaae/D < 0.5 -> compensate fz
Unitsmm
Used InHigh-speed machining, trochoidal milling, adaptive clearing

Why it matters

Radial engagement below full slot reduces chip thickness and underfeeds the cut if ignored.

Formula relationship

Compensate fz when ae < D so hm stays on target.

Typical mistake

Keeping slotting fz while programming a light ae.

How Chip Thinning Works

Programmed feed per tooth overstates chip thickness in light engagements; effective feed must increase to restore intended load.

Without correction, tools rub, heat rises, and tool life collapses despite “conservative” programmed feeds.

Key Variables

  • ae: Radial depth of cut.
  • D: Tool diameter.
  • fz_eff: Effective feed after thinning compensation.

Common Mistakes

  • Leaving CAM feed at full-slot values during finishing passes with small ae.
  • Compensating only for radial thinning while ignoring lead-angle thinning in some cutters.
