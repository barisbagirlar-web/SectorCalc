What is Heat Input t8/5?

Heat Input t8/5 is the cooling time between 800C and 500C used to estimate weld microstructure and hardness in the heat-affected zone. In engineering practice, it is used for WPS/PQR screening and to avoid brittle microstructures in high-strength steels.

PropertyValue
StandardEN 1011-2, AWS D1.1
Formulat8/5 = f(heat input, plate thickness, T0)
Unitsseconds
Used InWelding procedure qualification, HAZ control

Why it matters

Heat input windows on a WPS control metallurgy and distortion risk.

Formula relationship

HI = (V·I·60)/(1000·travel)·η.

Typical mistake

Mixing mm/min and in/min travel units.

How Heat Input t8/5 Works

Heat input is typically HI = (V·I·60)/(1000·travel speed) in kJ/mm for arc processes, with process efficiency applied when required.

t8/5 estimates how quickly the HAZ cools through the critical transformation range and is used in WPS/PQR screening.

Key Variables

  • HI: Arc energy delivered per unit length.
  • t8/5: Cooling time from 800 °C to 500 °C.
  • Efficiency: Process factor converting electrical energy to weld heat.

Common Mistakes

  • Omitting process efficiency when comparing MAG, TIG, and SAW heat inputs.
  • Using a single-pass formula for multi-pass joints without accounting for interpass temperature.
