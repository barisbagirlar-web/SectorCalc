What is Sling Angle Factor?

Sling Angle Factor is the reduction in rated capacity of a sling as the angle from vertical increases, calculated as the cosine of the angle. In engineering practice, it is used to verify safe working loads for multi-leg slings and lifting operations.

PropertyValue
StandardASME B30.9, EN 1492
FormulaSWL_actual = SWL_vertical * cos(theta)
UnitskN, lbf, tonne
Used InLifting, rigging, construction, offshore

How Sling Angle Factor Works

As sling angle from horizontal decreases, tension in each leg rises for the same payload.

Rigging charts publish angle derating; calculators convert hitch geometry into per-leg tension and utilization.

Key Variables

  • θ: Sling angle from horizontal or vertical, per chart convention.
  • Tension: Force in each sling leg.
  • WLL: Working load limit of the sling assembly.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading the wrong angle reference (from horizontal vs vertical) and applying the wrong derate.
  • Ignoring multi-leg load sharing when one leg is longer or the CG is offset.
