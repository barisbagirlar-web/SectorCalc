What is Bolt Preload?

Bolt Preload is the intentional tensile force applied to a bolt during tightening to create clamping force between joined parts. In engineering practice, it is used to prevent joint separation, leakage, and fatigue failure under external loads.

PropertyValue
StandardVDI 2230, ISO 16047
FormulaFM = k * tau * d2
UnitsN, kN
Used InBolted joints, flanges, pressure boundaries, structural connections

Why it matters

Clamp load — not torque alone — keeps joints closed under working load.

Formula relationship

T ≈ K·F·d for assembly screening.

Typical mistake

Copying dry nut factors onto lubricated hardware.

How Bolt Preload Works

Preload creates friction capacity and prevents separation under external tensile or shear loads.

Torque-to-preload conversion depends on thread and underhead friction; scatter bands must be considered for joint reliability.

Key Variables

  • T: Tightening torque.
  • K / μ: Nut factor or friction coefficients.
  • F_p: Achieved preload.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming torque alone guarantees preload without controlling lubrication and tool calibration.
  • Overlooking preload scatter — mean preload may be fine while the low tail separates.
