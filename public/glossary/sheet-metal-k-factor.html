What is Sheet Metal K-Factor?

Sheet Metal K-Factor is a ratio that locates the neutral axis in a bend relative to the material thickness, used to calculate bend allowance. In engineering practice, it is used to create accurate flat patterns for sheet metal parts before bending.

PropertyValue
StandardNone (empirical)
FormulaBA = (pi/180) * A * (R + K*T)
Unitsmm, inch
Used InSheet metal fabrication, CNC press brake programming

How Sheet Metal K-Factor Works

Bend allowance depends on thickness, inside radius, bend angle, and K-factor. Wrong K-factor creates wrong flat blanks.

K-factor is calibrated by material, tooling, and process; handbook defaults are only a starting point.

Key Variables

  • K: Neutral axis location as a fraction of thickness.
  • BA: Bend allowance added to flat pattern.
  • IR: Inside bend radius.

Common Mistakes

  • Using one K-factor for every material and radius — blanks become systematically long or short.
  • Forgetting to convert bend angle conventions (included vs complementary) before applying BA formulas.
