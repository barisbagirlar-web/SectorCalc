A SectorCalc case study is a reproducible record that separates measured shop data from calculated engineering previews, names the method and tool version, and states what was not measured. What counts as an industrial case study

In academic and industrial quality literature, a case study is not a testimonial. It is a bounded inquiry into a real decision context — a drawing freeze, a process capability risk, a weld procedure window, a quote margin — with enough methodological transparency that a peer can challenge the conclusion.

For SectorCalc, a publishable case study must answer five questions in plain language:

  1. Decision: What engineering or commercial decision was at stake?
  2. Method: Which standard, procedure, or documented method framed the analysis?
  3. Instrument: Which SectorCalc tool ID and engine version produced the calculated preview?
  4. Evidence split: Which quantities were measured on parts or processes, and which were calculated?
  5. Limits: What was deliberately not measured, and why that limit matters?

This definition aligns with how serious manufacturing organizations already treat DFMEA, process FMEA, capability studies, and design reviews: claims without method are opinion; claims with method but without measurement boundaries are incomplete; claims that conflate prediction with measurement are incorrect.

Why “ROI case studies” usually fail this gate

Software marketing often compresses a messy industrial story into a single percentage: scrap reduced, hours saved, margin gained. Those numbers can be true in a private ledger and still be non-citable on a public page if the method, sample frame, confounding factors, and measurement system analysis are absent. SectorCalc prefers an empty inventory over a persuasive fiction.

Measured data versus calculated previews

Industrial software pages frequently blur two epistemic categories. SectorCalc labels them explicitly in reports and requires the same split in every case study.

Category Definition Typical sources Allowed claim language
Measured Quantities obtained from physical parts, processes, or calibrated instruments under a stated MSA or procedure. CMM reports, SPC charts, torque transducers, weld coupons, time studies “Measured mean was…”, “Sample n=…”, “Gage R&R…”
Calculated preview Quantities predicted from drawing tolerances, standards tables, distributions, or costing models inside a deterministic engine. SectorCalc A1–A5 reports, ISO fits tables, RSS / Monte Carlo stacks “Predicted under assumptions…”, “Engineering preview…”, “Not measured SPC…”
Hybrid A study that uses measured inputs to bound a calculated model, or uses calculated risk to prioritize what to measure next. Tolerance risk screening before capability study; heat-input window before coupon plan Must label each quantity; never call predicted Cpk “process Cpk”

This distinction is not pedantry. A reviewer signing a PPAP package, a customer quality engineer, or an ISO auditor needs to know whether a number came from steel or from a model. SectorCalc calculator pages already print that warning; case studies must not erase it.

Determinism as a reproducibility requirement

When a study cites a Monte Carlo or statistical stack, the seed, distribution family, iteration count, and engine version must be listed so that a peer running the same inputs obtains the same hashes. Reproducibility is part of citability. Anecdotal “we ran it a few times” is not. See the glossary entries for Monte Carlo simulation, RSS tolerance, and process capability (Cpk).

A1–A5 audit architecture as evidence spine

Every SectorCalc engineering report is organized as an audit module. Case studies that cite SectorCalc must reference these sections rather than screenshots of marketing UI.

A1 — Engine identity
Tool ID, engine name, semantic version, and build markers that identify which code path executed.
A2 — Inputs
Entered values and canonical unit-normalized values so reviewers can see conversion as well as intent.
A3 — Applied formulas
The algebraic steps actually executed — not a paraphrased blog formula.
A4 — Assumptions and model boundaries
What the model pretends about independence, linearity, temperature, load sharing, or cost burden.
A5 — Warnings and limit checks
Where inputs leave validated ranges or where the verdict must not be over-read.

A case study that shows only a final number without A1–A5 is incomplete for this hub, even if the number is numerically correct. The audit trail is the citation apparatus.

Publication checklist (acceptance gate)

All items are mandatory. Partial packages are returned, not “soft published.”

  1. Named method (standard or documented internal procedure) with revision if applicable.
  2. SectorCalc tool ID and engine version (A1) matching the retained report.
  3. Input table with units and bounds sufficient for third-party reproduction (A2).
  4. Explicit split between measured quantities and calculated previews.
  5. A “what was not measured” paragraph with engineering rationale.
  6. Permission to publish from the data owner; anonymization rules if names or drawings are redacted.
  7. Conflict statement: consulting, employment, or commercial relationship with SectorCalc, if any.
  8. Retention: source files SectorCalc may archive for challenge review (redacted copies acceptable).

Optional but preferred: measurement system analysis summary for measured channels; before/after drawing excerpts with proprietary geometry redacted; link to a public or gated companion guide such as Tolerance Stack-Up Complete.

Domain protocols: tolerance, CNC, weld, costing

The following protocols describe how a future study in each domain should be structured. They are methodological templates — not evidence that such studies already exist on this site.

1. Tolerance stack-up and fits

Decision contexts typically include critical gap closure, press-fit retention, or interchangeability under ISO 286 classes. A strong study pairs drawing contributors with worst-case, RSS, and seeded Monte Carlo on the same stack, then states whether predicted risk triggered a measurement plan. Instruments: SC-008 Tolerance Stack-Up, ISO 286 Fits. Background: tolerance stack-up, worst-case analysis, RSS vs Monte Carlo.

2. CNC feeds, speeds, and cycle economics

Decision contexts include tool-life constrained parameters, chip thinning corrections, and cycle-time costing before quote release. Measured channels might include cutting-force samples or timed cycles; calculated channels include recommended surface speed, feed, and modeled cycle cost. Instruments: CNC Feeds & Speeds, Cycle Time Cost. Guide: CNC Optimization Complete.

3. Weld sizing and heat input

Decision contexts include throat sizing for load path and heat-input windows for procedure qualification. Coupon tests and NDT are measured; SectorCalc throat and heat-input reports are calculated previews that must not be labeled as qualified procedure results by themselves. Instruments: Weld Thickness, Weld Heat Input. Guide: Weld Sizing Complete.

4. Labor, machine rate, and quote margin

Decision contexts include burdened labor rate construction, machine-hour recovery, and full-cost quote margin. Time studies and payroll burden factors are measured or accounting-derived; SectorCalc models are calculated structures that make assumptions visible (A4). Instruments: True Labor Cost, Machine Hour Rate, Quote Pricing. Guide: Labor Costing Complete.

Illustrative protocol (not a published study)

Label: ILLUSTRATIVE PROTOCOL ONLY. Synthetic teaching numbers. Not a customer outcome. Not indexed as a case study row in the inventory table above.

Suppose a design review freezes a one-dimensional assembly gap with six tolerance contributors. The team wants to know whether worst-case closure is impossible while RSS still looks comfortable — a classic false calm.

Protocol steps

  1. Export contributor limits from the drawing into a reproducible input table (nominal, bilateral or unilateral limits, units).
  2. Run SC-008 worst-case, RSS, and seeded Monte Carlo with a recorded seed and iteration count.
  3. Retain the A1–A5 report hashes.
  4. If predicted tail risk exceeds the internal threshold, open a measurement plan on the two highest-sensitivity contributors — do not claim scrap savings yet.
  5. Only after measured data returns may a hybrid study compare predicted sensitivity ranking with observed contributor variance.

What this protocol deliberately does not claim

  • No “Company X saved $Y.”
  • No “Cpk improved from A to B” without measured SPC.
  • No competitor accuracy ranking.

The protocol’s only job is to show the minimum narrative structure a real study must follow. When a partner completes those steps with permission to publish, the inventory table gains its first row.

Relation to industrial quality literature

Classical manufacturing quality systems already encode the same epistemic discipline this hub demands. Process FMEA separates severity, occurrence, and detection with explicit assumptions. Capability studies require sample size, subgrouping logic, and stability checks before a Cpk number is trusted. Design reviews ask for risk ranking before capital is spent on tooling. A public case study that skips those disciplines is not “marketing shorthand” — it is a category error.

SectorCalc’s role in that literature is narrow and honest: provide deterministic, formula-visible engineering previews with audit trails so teams can decide what to measure next and which drawing limits to reopen. The platform is not a laboratory information system, not a QMS repository, and not a substitute for a licensed professional engineer’s seal. Case studies that imply otherwise are rewritten or rejected.

Vocabulary discipline

  • Predicted Cpk — model output under stated distributions; never “process Cpk” without measured SPC.
  • Engineering preview — SectorCalc’s default report label for calculated results.
  • Contributor sensitivity — which stack members dominate predicted variance; useful for measurement prioritization.
  • Model boundary — A4 statement of what the engine assumes and where it stops.

Measurement system expectations for measured channels

When a study includes measured data, reviewers will ask whether the measurement system can resolve the effect being claimed. SectorCalc does not require a full AIAG MSA manual as an attachment for every study, but it does require enough MSA honesty to prevent false precision:

  • Instrument type and resolution for each measured channel.
  • Sample size and sampling frame (which lots, which shifts, which fixtures).
  • Whether gage R&R or equivalent was performed when the claim depends on small deltas.
  • Environmental or setup factors known to matter (temperature for fits, fixturing for CMM).

If MSA is incomplete, the study may still publish as a decision narrative that stops at “predicted risk triggered measurement,” but it may not publish a before/after capability victory lap. That rule keeps the hub aligned with how competent quality engineers already read vendor claims.

Automatic rejection criteria

Submissions are rejected without soft edits when any of the following appear:

  • Unnamed “Fortune 500” or “major OEM” claims without permission path.
  • Percentage ROI, scrap dollars, or headcount reductions without method and sample frame.
  • Screenshots without A1 engine version or reproducible inputs.
  • Calling predicted Monte Carlo percentiles “measured capability.”
  • Competitor accuracy benchmarks invented for marketing contrast.
  • AI-generated narrative that cannot produce source files on challenge.

Rejection is a quality feature. It protects engineers who already distrust vendor case-study pages, and it protects SectorCalc’s glossary, guide, and calculator surfaces from contamination by unverifiable claims.

How reviewers should read a study

Procurement and quality reviewers can use this reading order:

  1. Read the decision and method first — if either is vague, stop.
  2. Check the measured/calculated split table — if missing, treat numbers as non-citable.
  3. Verify A1 version against the retained report.
  4. Read A4/A5 for model boundaries and warnings.
  5. Only then evaluate whether the conclusion is proportionate to the evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Why are there zero published SectorCalc case studies?

Because this hub refuses invented ROI, unnamed customers, and scrap-dollar claims. A study appears only after method, tool version, reproducible inputs, measured-versus-calculated split, and publication permission clear the evidence checklist.

Are SectorCalc calculator outputs measured data?

No. Calculator outputs are engineering previews predicted from drawing tolerances, standards, and stated distributions. They are not a substitute for measured SPC, manufacturer ratings, governing-code compliance, or licensed engineering sign-off.

What makes an industrial case study citable for this hub?

A citable study names the method or standard, the SectorCalc tool ID and engine version, units and input bounds a third party can reproduce, a clear split between measured shop data and calculated previews, an explicit not-measured paragraph, and documented permission to publish.

Can I use SectorCalc reports in PPAP or customer audits?

Use them to prioritize capability studies, justify tolerance changes before tooling freeze, and attach A1–A5 audit trails as engineering rationale. Do not present predicted Cpk or Monte Carlo tails as measured process capability unless the study also includes measured SPC from production parts.

How do I submit a candidate case study?

Email support@sectorcalc.com with subject Case study candidate, answers to the evidence checklist, and source files you are willing to cite. Marketing decks without source data are rejected.

Is the illustrative protocol a real customer story?

No. It is a teaching scaffold labeled ILLUSTRATIVE PROTOCOL ONLY. It will never appear as a row in the published inventory table.
