Complete Guide to Tolerance Stack-Up

The problem: You are about to freeze tolerances on a drawing — and the only question that matters is whether the gap still closes when every contributor hits its legal extreme.

What you get: Get a defendable stack prediction before steel is cut.

Explanation — decision this supports

Decide whether a proposed tolerance set can close a critical gap, which contributors dominate risk, and whether worst-case, RSS, or Monte Carlo is the right release criterion for the drawing.

Required inputs

Each contributor needs a nominal, bilateral tolerance, sign/direction, and distribution model. The assembly needs upper/lower specification limits, iteration count, and a seed when Monte Carlo is used.

Methodology — formula & method

Stack nominal X = Σ nᵢ. Worst-case spread sums extremes. RSS uses √(Σ tᵢ²) under independent centered normals. Monte Carlo draws from assigned distributions with a fixed seed for audit reproducibility.

What the result means

Higher predicted Cpk means more margin to the gap limits under your distribution assumptions. A large WC–RSS gap signals rare simultaneous extremes. If Monte Carlo disagrees with RSS, at least one distribution is poorly approximated by ±3σ normal.

What moves the result

The largest tolerances and the loosest distributions move predicted PPM the most. Tightening a small contributor rarely helps if a single wide uniform contributor dominates the stack.

Assumptions

Contributors are independent along one measurement axis. Distributions you assign are honest models of process behavior. Form, orientation, and 2D position tolerances are outside this model.

Model boundaries

Screening calculation for 1D linear stacks only. Not a GD&T datum-frame solver. Predicted Cpk is not measured SPC. Does not replace a licensed engineer’s drawing release judgment.

Common engineering mistakes

Treating every drawing ± block as uniform; mixing absolute and deviation limits; forgetting sign direction; trusting Monte Carlo without fixing the seed for audits.

Uses classical WC/RSS stack arithmetic and statistical tolerancing ideas aligned with common ASME Y14.5 practice. It does not claim full Y14.5 compliance for complex datum schemes.

A 1D linear tolerance stack-up sums independent dimensional contributors along a single measurement direction to predict whether an assembly gap, interference, or overall length will stay within specification. SectorCalc SC-008 compares worst-case, root-sum-square (RSS), and seeded Monte Carlo methods on the same stack, reports predicted Cpk and PPM from your chosen distributions, and flags engineering-preview limits before you commit tooling. This is a design-time model — not a substitute for measured statistical process control (SPC) on production parts.

What is a 1D tolerance stack-up?

In mechanical design, critical functional dimensions — bearing preload, seal compression, valve lash, shaft end float — rarely depend on a single feature. They emerge from a chain of part lengths, bore depths, shim thicknesses, and clearance gaps measured along one axis. A 1D linear stack models that chain as a signed sum of nominal values plus tolerances.

Each contributor carries a nominal dimension, a ± tolerance band, and a sign indicating whether the feature adds or subtracts from the closed loop. The assembly specification is expressed as a deviation band around the nominal stack sum — for example, a gap must remain between −0.150 mm and +0.150 mm relative to the target closure dimension.

Stack nominal:   Xnom = Σ ni
Worst-case envelope:   XWC = Σ (ni ± ti) arranged for maximum accumulation
RSS spread:   TRSS = √(Σ ti²)

SC-008 operates strictly in one dimension. It does not resolve two-dimensional position tolerances, profile variations, or form errors that require a full GD&T datum reference frame. Treat it as the first-pass arithmetic check that every stack-up analyst performs before moving to more complex tolerance analysis software or physical build-and-measure validation.

💡
Stack contributors often come from machined features whose process capability you already model elsewhere. Cross-check nominal and tolerance inputs against SC-020 Feeds & Speeds and your shop's capability studies before accepting a predicted Cpk.

Worst-case, RSS, and Monte Carlo

Three accumulation philosophies answer the same question — how much total variation should I budget? — with different assumptions about part-to-part independence and process centering.

Worst-case (WC)

Worst-case analysis assumes every contributor simultaneously sits at its least favorable extreme within the stated tolerance. For a stack of n independent bilateral tolerances, the accumulated spread equals the arithmetic sum of individual tolerances. WC is conservative: it guarantees assembly feasibility if and only if the stack closes under simultaneous extremes. The trade-off is over-design — you may reject stacks that would pass in production because simultaneous extremes are statistically rare.

TWC = t1 + t2 + … + tn

Root-sum-square (RSS)

RSS assumes independent, centered normal variation where each tolerance represents approximately ±3σ of that contributor. Variances add; standard deviations do not. RSS predicts a ~99.73% stack envelope under ideal normality and independence — the classical ASME Y14.5 statistical tolerancing shortcut. It is less conservative than WC and appropriate when processes are capable, centered, and uncorrelated.

TRSS = √(t1² + t2² + … + tn²)

Monte Carlo simulation

When distributions are non-normal, truncated, or mixed across components, RSS closed-form math loses accuracy. Monte Carlo draws random samples per contributor according to the assigned distribution, sums them, and builds an empirical histogram of stack outcomes. SC-008 runs 10,000 seeded iterations using Decimal arithmetic, reports the 99.7% spread (P0.13 to P99.87), predicted PPM outside specification limits, and a 95% confidence interval on the defect rate.

Compare all three methods side by side. If RSS and Monte Carlo agree closely, your distribution assumptions are self-consistent. A large gap between RSS and Monte Carlo signals that at least one contributor is not well approximated by the ±3σ normal model — revisit distribution assignments before trusting predicted Cpk.

Distribution choices per component

SC-008 assigns a distribution to each stack contributor. The tolerance field semantics depend on the distribution type — a detail that causes silent errors if ignored.

  • Normal: tolerance = ±3σ. The process is assumed centered on nominal with Gaussian spread. Appropriate for capable machining and grinding when the drawing tolerance reflects natural process variation rather than a hard physical limit.
  • Truncated normal: same ±3σ interpretation, but samples are rejected outside ±tolerance. Use when inspection or tooling physically prevents out-of-limit parts from entering the stack — for example, a selective assembly station or 100% screening.
  • Uniform: tolerance = hard half-range. Every value between nominal − t and nominal + t is equally likely. Models worst-case-unaware processes with no central tendency — rough casting wall thickness, uncontrolled shim packs, or legacy ± tolerance blocks without capability data.
  • Triangular: tolerance = hard half-range with the mode at nominal. A pragmatic expert-elicitation shape when you know the minimum, maximum, and most likely value but lack sample data. More conservative than normal at the tails, less conservative than uniform.

Choosing the wrong distribution is the single largest source of optimistic Cpk in stack-up work. A drawing ±0.05 mm on a turned feature is not automatically uniform simply because the print shows a bilateral block — if the lathe holds Cpk ≥ 1.33, normal or truncated-normal is more defensible. Document your rationale; SC-008 outputs the distribution set in the report hash for audit traceability.

Predicted Cpk vs measured SPC

Process capability index Cpk measures how well a stable process fits inside specification limits:

Cpk = min [ (USL − μ) / 3σ , (μ − LSL) / 3σ ]

In SC-008, μ and σ are model-derived from your tolerance inputs and distribution choices — not from an X̄-R chart, individuals chart, or production CMM dataset. The reported value is predicted Cpk: a design estimate of what capability you need (or what capability your assumed distributions imply) for the stack to meet the target.

Measured SPC Cpk from AIAG or ISO 22514 methods requires:

  • A defined, stable process in statistical control
  • Adequate sample size (typically ≥ 100 individual measurements for initial studies)
  • Rational subgrouping that reflects the actual sources of variation
  • Specification limits that match the functional requirement, not just the drawing block

Do not cite SC-008 predicted Cpk on a PPAP cover sheet or customer capability report. Use it to prioritize which contributors to tighten, which processes to capability-study first, and whether the stack has margin before cutting steel. When production data arrives, replace assumed distributions with empirical histograms or fitted parameters and re-run.

ISO 286 and drawing tolerances

ISO 286-1 defines the ISO tolerance system for linear sizes — fundamental deviations (e.g., H7, g6) and tolerance grades IT01 through IT18. When a stack contributor is a standard fit — a ⌀32 mm H7 bore mating a ⌀32 mm f7 shaft — the ± deviation you enter should come from the ISO 286 tables for the nominal size and grade, not from an approximate round number.

Key points when mapping ISO 286 to SC-008 inputs:

  • Upper and lower deviations are asymmetric for most letter codes. Convert each feature to a signed nominal plus bilateral tolerance only after applying the correct deviation values relative to the stack direction.
  • Grade IT drives precision cost. IT7 versus IT9 on the same nominal can swing a marginal stack from pass to fail under WC while RSS remains acceptable — run both methods before relaxing a grade.
  • Fit class ≠ stack sign. A shaft diameter and its mating bore contribute opposite signs to an internal clearance stack. ISO 286 tells you the size limits; you still assign the sign per the stack sketch.

SC-008 references ISO 286-1 as context for deviation values. It does not auto-lookup grade tables — you remain responsible for entering the correct numeric deviations from the standard or your CAD model's tolerance advisor.

Seed determinism and reproducibility

Monte Carlo results must be reproducible for engineering review, customer audit, and regression testing. SC-008 uses a seeded linear congruential generator (LCG) with Decimal arithmetic: identical inputs, distribution selections, seed, and engine version produce identical samples, histograms, PPM, and report hashes.

Practical implications:

  • Record the seed in your calculation log alongside drawing revision and engine version string.
  • Changing the seed while holding inputs constant explores Monte Carlo sampling noise — useful for sensitivity checks but not for baseline sign-off.
  • Share URLs and exported reports embed input and output integrity hashes so a reviewer can confirm the report matches the stated inputs without re-entering data manually.

Determinism applies to the simulation engine, not to physical manufacturing. Two production lots with the same seed in SC-008 will not produce identical parts — the seed governs only the pseudo-random draw sequence inside the calculator.

When worst-case is required vs statistical methods

Statistical stack-up (RSS or Monte Carlo) is appropriate when:

  • Contributors are independently manufactured and not sorted into correlated sets
  • Processes are centered and reasonably capable (Cpk ≥ 1.0, ideally ≥ 1.33)
  • Regulatory or contractual requirements permit statistical tolerancing per ASME Y14.5
  • Failure mode is gradual ( increased wear, noise) rather than sudden and safety-critical at the tolerance boundary

Worst-case (or worst-case with a small statistical guard band) is required when:

  • Safety-critical or single-failure-path mechanisms where simultaneous extremes must not occur — flight controls, medical device interference fits subject to FDA worst-case reasoning, pressure-boundary preload stacks
  • Small lot sizes or prototype builds where the central limit theorem provides no comfort
  • Customer contract, drawing note, or PPAP procedure explicitly mandates WC accumulation
  • Contributors are intentionally correlated — matched pairs, selective assembly, or serial size compensation
  • Any contributor lacks capability data and you cannot defend a distribution assumption

Best practice: run WC first. If WC fails, the stack is not robust regardless of RSS optimism. If WC passes with excessive margin, RSS and Monte Carlo quantify potential tolerance relief or cost savings from loosening non-critical contributors.

Common failure modes

Direction sign errors

The most frequent stack-up mistake is assigning the wrong sign to a contributor. A bore depth that removes material from the stack length must subtract; a spacer adds. Work from a single closed-loop sketch with a consistent positive direction. One sign inversion can flip a passing stack to a critical failure under WC while leaving RSS deceptively unchanged if the inverted term is small.

Bonus tolerance in 1D linear stacks

Under ASME Y14.5, bonus tolerance arises when a feature-of-size departs from MMC — extra clearance from a hole growing or a shaft shrinking. SC-008 1D linear analysis does not automatically apply MMC/LMC bonus or shift tolerances. Enter the fixed geometric tolerance or size tolerance appropriate to the analysis state (typically at MMC for minimum clearance stacks, or at nominal for a neutral estimate). Attempting to fold bonus tolerance into a simple ± value without a full datum reference frame overstates available margin.

Datum scheme mismatches

A stack-up sketch defines an measurement path between two functional surfaces. GD&T datum features (primary, secondary, tertiary) define a different constraint model. If your stack direction is not aligned with the datum-controlled degree of freedom — for example, stacking axial lengths while flatness and perpendicularity govern seating — the 1D result may not bound the true assembly variation. Resolve datum precedence on the drawing before trusting a single-axis sum.

Other recurring errors

  • Double-counting: including the same variation twice under different feature names (CAD dimension and drawing callout).
  • Temperature and compliance: ignoring thermal expansion or gasket creep that consumes part of the spec band at operating condition.
  • Confusing spec limits with tolerance: SC-008 spec deviation is relative to the nominal stack sum; USL/LSL are computed internally from that sum plus your entered deviations.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use worst-case instead of RSS for a tolerance stack-up?

Use worst-case when the drawing, contract, or safety analysis requires guaranteed closure under simultaneous tolerance extremes — common in aerospace, medical devices, and any assembly where a single stacked gap controls a failure mode. RSS is appropriate when parts are independently produced by capable, centered processes and your quality system accepts statistical tolerancing per ASME Y14.5. SC-008 runs both on the same inputs so you can see whether statistical relief is even available before proposing tolerance changes to manufacturing.

What is the difference between normal and uniform distribution in SC-008?

For normal and truncated-normal distributions, the entered ± tolerance represents three standard deviations (±3σ) centered on nominal. For uniform and triangular distributions, the tolerance is the hard half-range — every value within that band is possible. Using normal when the process is actually uncontrolled yields optimistic Cpk; using uniform for a capable lathe wastes margin. Match the distribution to the manufacturing evidence you have, not to the shape of the tolerance symbol on the print.

Can I use SC-008 predicted Cpk on a PPAP submission?

No. SC-008 predicted Cpk is computed from assumed distributions and drawing tolerances — it is a design-time forecast, not measured process capability. PPAP and customer SPC requirements expect Cpk from production data in statistical control. Use SC-008 to identify which contributors most need capability studies and whether the stack has predicted margin before you cut tooling; replace model assumptions with measured mean and standard deviation once parts exist.

Why does Monte Carlo use a fixed seed, and should I change it?

The seed fixes the pseudo-random sequence so two engineers with identical inputs reproduce the same histogram, PPM, and report hashes — essential for review, audit, and regression testing. Change the seed only when you intentionally want to test sampling stability or demonstrate that conclusions do not depend on a particular draw sequence. For baseline documentation, keep the default or assigned seed constant and record it in your calculation log.

Does SC-008 include GD&T bonus tolerance in 1D stack-up?

No. Bonus tolerance from MMC/LMC departure applies within a datum reference frame and feature-of-size rules — it cannot be faithfully captured in a simple signed 1D sum without over-counting margin. Enter the tolerance appropriate to your analysis condition (typically at MMC for minimum clearance checks). For stacks where bonus tolerance dominates — sheet metal slots to tabs, clearance holes to pins at LMC — use dedicated GD&T stack software or manual two-zone analysis rather than inflating a linear ± value.
