1. What is Tolerance Stack-Up?

A tolerance stack-up is a 1D linear analysis that sums independent dimensional contributors to predict whether an assembly gap, interference, or overall length will stay within specification. It is the foundation of statistical tolerancing and the starting point for GD&T-based variation analysis.

In mechanical design, critical functional dimensions rarely depend on a single feature. They emerge from a chain of part lengths, bore depths, shim thicknesses, and clearance gaps. A stack-up models that chain as a signed sum of nominal values plus tolerances.

Without stack-up analysis, designers either over-tolerize (driving cost) or under-tolerize (driving scrap and rework). The deterministic approach guarantees that two engineers with the same inputs always get the same result -- essential for audit and customer review.

5. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1: Wrong sign assignment

A bore depth that removes material must subtract, not add. One sign inversion can flip a passing stack to critical failure under worst-case while leaving RSS deceptively unchanged.

Fix: Always work from a single closed-loop sketch with a consistent positive direction. Double-check every sign against the stack sketch before calculating.

Mistake 2: Using RSS for non-normal processes

A drawing plus-minus 0.05 mm on a turned feature is not automatically uniform. If the lathe holds Cpk greater than or equal to 1.33, normal is more defensible. Using RSS for uncontrolled processes yields optimistic Cpk.

Fix: Match the distribution to manufacturing evidence, not the tolerance symbol shape. Document your rationale in the A4 assumptions section.

Mistake 3: Confusing predicted Cpk with measured Cpk

SC-008 predicted Cpk is a design estimate from assumed distributions. Citing it on a PPAP cover sheet violates AIAG requirements.

Fix: Use predicted Cpk to prioritize capability studies. Replace assumptions with measured X-bar-R chart data once production parts exist.
