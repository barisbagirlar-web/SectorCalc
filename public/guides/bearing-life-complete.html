1. What is Bearing Life (ISO 281)?

ISO 281 bearing life calculation predicts how long a rolling bearing will operate under given load, speed, and lubrication conditions. The standard provides life adjustment factors that bridge the gap between laboratory tests and real-world reliability.

The basic rating life L10 assumes ideal lubrication and cleanliness. In practice, contamination and inadequate viscosity reduce life. The aISO factor adjusts L10 to Lnm based on operating conditions.

Accurate bearing life prediction prevents both premature failure (costly downtime) and over-specification (unnecessary cost). The deterministic engine ensures traceable inputs for warranty and maintenance interval justification.

2. Applicable Standards

  • ISO 281:2007: Dynamic load ratings and rating life calculation for rolling bearings, including modified life factors.
  • ISO 4406: Contamination coding for lubricating oil cleanliness that feeds eC contamination factors.
  • DIN 51825: Lubricating grease specifications affecting viscosity and film formation in bearing service.

3. Methodology & Formulas

This guide explains how to calculate adjusted bearing life using the aISO methodology. The deterministic engine handles the digitized aISO equations with automatic capping at 50.

3.1 Basic Rating Life L10

L10 = (C/P)^p

Basic rating life from dynamic capacity and equivalent load. Exponent p is 3 for ball bearings and 10/3 for roller bearings.

3.2 Viscosity Ratio kappa

kappa = nu / nu1

Viscosity ratio comparing actual lubricant viscosity to the required viscosity for adequate film thickness.

3.3 Life Adjustment Factor aISO

aISO = f(kappa, eC, Cu/P)

Life modification factor combining lubrication quality, contamination, and fatigue load limit ratio.

5. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1: Using aISO > 50 without questioning

The digitized aISO equations cap at 50 for a reason. Excessive optimism in viscosity or contamination assumptions leads to unrealistic life predictions.

Fix: If aISO > 50, verify kappa > 2 and contamination eC < 0.5. Otherwise, use aISO = 50 as upper bound.

Mistake 2: Ignoring temperature effect on viscosity

Oil viscosity drops 50 percent for every 10C rise. Using room-temperature viscosity for a 90C bearing housing underestimates kappa significantly.

Fix: Always use viscosity at operating temperature. The calculator includes a temperature-viscosity correction.

Mistake 3: Using catalog C without considering mounting

Catalog C assumes perfect inner ring support. Loose fits or poor housing geometry reduce effective load capacity.

Fix: Apply mounting correction factors from ISO 281 Annex B. Document in A4 assumptions.
