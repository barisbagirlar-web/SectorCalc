1. What is CNC Optimization?

CNC optimization is the process of selecting cutting parameters that maximize productivity while maintaining part quality and tool life. It bridges the gap between theoretical metal cutting science and practical shop-floor economics.

The Taylor tool life equation (V*T^n = C) relates cutting speed to tool life through empirical constants. Chip thinning occurs when radial engagement is less than half the tool diameter, requiring feed compensation to maintain chip load.

Optimizing CNC parameters can reduce tooling costs by 20-30 percent and cycle times by 15-25 percent. The deterministic approach ensures that feed/speed recommendations are traceable to specific tool geometry and workpiece material.

5. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1: Using catalog C and n values without calibration

Catalog values are starting points, not contracts. Your machine, coolant, and setup differ from the test conditions.

Fix: Calibrate C and n from your own tool-wear data. SC-020 includes a calibration mode for this.

Mistake 2: Ignoring chip thinning in high-speed machining

Adaptive clearing and trochoidal paths use low ae/D ratios. Without compensation, chip load drops below the effective range, causing rubbing and premature wear.

Fix: Always apply chip thinning factor when ae/D < 0.5. The calculator does this automatically.

Mistake 3: Exceeding machine power for marginal gain

A 10 percent speed increase that requires 30 percent more power is not economical if it triggers spindle overload alarms.

Fix: Use the power check in the calculator. Optimize for cost per part, not just cycle time.
