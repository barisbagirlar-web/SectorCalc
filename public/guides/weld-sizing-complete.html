1. What is Weld Sizing?

Weld sizing is the process of determining adequate weld dimensions to carry design loads while minimizing distortion and residual stress. It bridges structural requirements with fabrication practicality.

The 0.707 rule assumes a 45 degree equal-leg fillet. Real throat dimensions depend on penetration, convexity, and inspection method. Heat input controls microstructure and hardness in the heat-affected zone.

Undersized welds fail structurally. Oversized welds waste material, increase distortion, and may introduce brittle microstructures. The deterministic calculator balances these constraints with visible formulas.

2. Applicable Standards

  • AWS D1.1: Structural welding code requirements for fillet sizing, inspection, and allowable stresses in steel construction.
  • ISO 5817: Quality levels for imperfections in fusion-welded joints used in fabrication acceptance.
  • EN 1011-2: European guidance for arc welding of ferritic steels, including heat-input and hydrogen control practice.

5. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1: Assuming 0.707 for all fillet types

The 0.707 rule applies only to 45 degree equal-leg fillets. Convex fillets have larger throat; concave fillets may have smaller effective throat.

Fix: Measure actual throat with ultrasonic or fillet gauge. Enter measured value, not theoretical.

Mistake 2: Ignoring penetration in strength calc

AWS D1.1 allows credit for penetration only when demonstrated by procedure qualification. Assuming penetration without PQR is unsafe.

Fix: Use nominal throat (0.707 * leg) for design. Add penetration bonus only with qualified WPS/PQR.

Mistake 3: Excessive heat input on high-strength steel

High heat input (>2.0 kJ/mm) on Q&T steels softens the HAZ and reduces fatigue life.

Fix: Use SC-029 to check t8/5 cooling time. Limit heat input per EN 1011-2 recommendations for steel grade.
