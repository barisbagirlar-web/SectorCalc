1. What is True Labor Costing?

True labor costing is the process of calculating the fully loaded cost of an hour of labor, including all direct and indirect costs that the employer actually pays.

Net salary is only 40-60 percent of true labor cost. The remainder includes payroll taxes, health insurance, retirement contributions, paid time off, training, and shop overhead allocation.

Understating labor cost leads to unprofitable quotes. Overstating it loses bids. The deterministic calculator breaks down every component with visible formulas.

5. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1: Using net salary as labor cost

Net salary is what the employee receives, not what the employer pays. Using net salary for quoting guarantees under-billing.

Fix: Always use gross salary plus burden. The calculator shows the difference explicitly.

Mistake 2: Ignoring non-productive time

An employee with 15 days PTO, 10 holidays, and 5 training days has only about 220 billable days. Using 260 days overstates capacity by 18 percent.

Fix: Enter all non-productive time in the calculator. It automatically reduces billable hours.

Mistake 3: Averaging overhead across all employees

A senior machinist using a $200K CNC machine should carry more overhead than a junior inspector. Averaging distorts both quotes.

Fix: Use activity-based costing where possible. The calculator supports per-role overhead allocation.
