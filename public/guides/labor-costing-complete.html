True labor cost is what an employer actually spends to keep one worker productive for one hour — not the net salary on a payslip. SC-010 grosses up take-home pay, adds statutory employer contributions, benefits, severance accrual, overtime, and amortized recruitment, then returns a conservation-locked monthly total and a fully loaded hourly rate suitable for job costing and quoting.

Why net salary is not your cost

Shop owners and estimators often anchor on the number the employee sees: monthly net pay. That figure excludes employee-side income tax and social deductions already withheld, and it completely ignores employer-side statutory contributions, health premiums, meal and transport allowances, bonus accruals, severance provisions, overtime premiums, and hiring costs spread over tenure.

When you quote a weldment or machined part using net pay divided by 160 hours, you systematically under-recover cost. The gap — sometimes called hidden labor cost — routinely runs 30–80% above net depending on jurisdiction and benefit package. SC-010 makes that gap explicit with a line-item breakdown and a cost multiplier (true monthly cost ÷ net monthly).

Net to gross salary conversion

Most payroll systems work from gross; estimators often know net. SC-010 reverses the employee burden with a simplified gross-up:

grossMonthly = netMonthly / (1 − employeeRate)

Where employeeRate is the combined employee-side deduction ratio (income tax + employee social contributions). The tool accepts net salary as hourly, weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly and normalizes to a monthly net first using 4.33 weeks per month (52 ÷ 12).

netMonthly (hourly) = netHourly × hoursPerWeek × 4.33

The gross-up is a planning estimate, not a tax return. Progressive tax brackets, caps, and allowances are not modeled in v1.0.0. For a single effective rate that matches observed payslips, adjust employeeRate manually until gross reconciles with payroll.

Employer social security and unemployment

After gross salary is estimated, employer statutory costs apply on the gross base (not net):

employerSS = gross × employerSSRate employerUnemployment = gross × employerUnempRate

In the United States reference preset, employer FICA (6.2% + 1.45%) and an approximate FUTA/SUTA unemployment burden are represented. Germany, France, Turkey, and other presets carry different combined employer rates. Wage bases, ceilings, and experience-rated unemployment taxes are not fully modeled — the rates are reference estimates for engineering preview only.

Benefits and allowances

Employer-paid benefits enter as fixed monthly amounts plus annual bonus spread over twelve months:

benefitsMonthly = health + meal + transport + (annualBonus / 12)

Health insurance, meal vouchers, and commuting allowances are common in EU and Turkish shops; US shops often add employer health premiums separately from gross. Bonus accrual smooths a lump-sum payment into monthly job cost — useful for quoting even when bonus is discretionary.

💡
After SC-010 gives you trueHourlyCost, paste it into the labor rate field in SC-012 Quote Pricing — do not re-use net hourly pay. For machine burden (depreciation, maintenance, floor space), use a separate machine hourly rate or shop overhead in SC-012.

Severance accrual

In jurisdictions with statutory severance (e.g. Turkey: roughly one month gross per year of service), SC-010 accrues severance monthly as a ratio of gross:

severanceMonthly = gross × severanceRate

For Turkey the reference severanceRate is 0.0833 (1 ÷ 12 of one month's gross per year). US and UK presets use zero statutory severance; contractual redundancy or WARN costs are not auto-included. Treat severance accrual as a long-run average — actual payout depends on tenure at separation.

Burden rate and cost multiplier

The burden rate (or labor burden) is the ratio of total employer cost to gross or to net, depending on convention. SC-010 reports:

costMultiplier = trueMonthlyCost / netMonthly hiddenCostPct = (trueMonthlyCost − netMonthly) / netMonthly × 100

A multiplier of 1.65 means the employer spends 65% more than the employee's take-home. This is the number production controllers watch when net wages rise but quotes stay flat. Overtime and recruitment amortization inflate the multiplier further when those inputs are non-zero.

Fully loaded hourly cost

The hourly rate used in routing sheets and CNC time studies should include every monthly component divided by productive hours:

trueHourlyCost = trueMonthlyCost / (hoursPerWeek × 4.33)

Overtime is modeled separately: average monthly overtime hours × base hourly × overtime multiplier (default 1.5×). Base hourly for overtime is derived from gross, not from true hourly — a conservative simplification. Paid leave, training, and non-productive time are not subtracted from the denominator in v1.0.0; if only 85% of paid hours are billable, divide true monthly cost by billable hours instead, or inflate overhead in SC-012.

trueMonthlyCost = gross + employerSS + employerUnemp + benefits + severance + overtime + recruitmentMonthly

The engine enforces conservation: the sum of breakdown line items must equal true monthly cost within 1×10⁻⁹; a mismatch throws an error rather than shipping a wrong total.

Country-rate caveats

Built-in presets (US, TR, DE, GB, IN, FR) ship reference rates as Decimal strings for reproducibility. They are starting points from public summaries, not legal advice:

  • US — FICA employer 7.65%; FUTA ~6% on first $7k is averaged, not wage-base capped.
  • TR — SGK employer ~20.5%; severance accrual 8.33%/month of gross.
  • DE / FR — High employer social ratios; local surcharges and industry funds omitted.
  • GB — Employer NIC 13.8% above threshold simplified to flat ratio.
  • IN — EPF/ESI approximate; gratuity after five years not accrued.

Always reconcile one employee against actual payroll before rolling rates across the shop. Update inputs when law changes; SC-010 does not fetch live statutory tables.

Using loaded labor in quotes

Job quoting requires fully absorbed labor: loaded hourly × run hours, plus setup at the same or a higher setup rate, plus scrap, overhead, and margin. Workflow:

  1. Run SC-010 for each trade or use a blended shop rate (weighted average of true hourly costs).
  2. Copy trueHourlyCost into SC-012 as laborHourlyCost.
  3. Add machine hours at machine burden, material with scrap yield, shop overhead rate, and target margin on sell price.
  4. For tolerance-sensitive or weld-critical jobs, cross-check process assumptions with SC-008 Tolerance Stack-Up and SC-001 Weld Thickness.

Never mix net pay in SC-012 and loaded pay in overhead — that double-counts or under-counts depending on how overhead was calibrated. Pick one labor philosophy per quote and stay consistent.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between burden rate and cost multiplier in SC-010?

SC-010's costMultiplier is true monthly employer cost divided by net monthly pay. A burden rate in cost accounting sometimes means employer cost divided by gross wages only. Both express how much extra you pay above a reference wage; always note which base (net vs gross) your ERP uses before comparing numbers.

Why does SC-010 use 4.33 weeks per month?

52 weeks ÷ 12 months = 4.333… weeks. Using 4.33 keeps hourly normalization deterministic and aligned with the engine's Decimal constants. Some payroll systems use 4.35 or calendar days; if your shop standard differs, adjust hours per week or scale the result.

Can I use SC-010 output directly in SC-012 Quote Pricing?

Yes — that is the intended handoff. Enter SC-010's trueHourlyCost as SC-012's labor hourly cost. SC-012 does not auto-fetch SC-010; rates must be copied manually so you control blending and revision dates.

Are country presets legally certified payroll rates?

No. Presets are reference estimates for engineering preview and quoting sensitivity. Wage bases, caps, industry funds, and progressive tax are simplified. Verify against your payroll provider and local law before contractual or statutory use.

How should overtime affect my quoted hourly rate?

Enter average monthly overtime hours and multiplier in SC-010 to bake overtime into true hourly cost, or keep overtime out and add a contingency line in SC-012. Mixing both without adjusting hours double-counts. For spike workloads, run a what-if with higher overtime hours before committing a fixed shop rate.

SC-012 conservation-locks nine cost components so the breakdown always sums to total cost before margin is applied.

Full absorption quoting model

Variable costing quotes only direct material and direct labor. Full absorption (full costing) also allocates shop overhead and financing so the quote recovers fixed rent, supervision, QA, and working-capital cost. SC-012 implements a practical job-shop stack:

  1. Effective material (scrap-adjusted)
  2. Direct labor, machine, and setup time at hourly burdens
  3. Energy, consumables, and shipping as lump direct costs
  4. Overhead as a rate on direct cost
  5. Finance charge from customer payment days
  6. Sell price from target margin
totalCost = effMaterial + labor + machine + setup + energy + consumables + shipping + overhead + finance

The runtime engine verifies that these nine items sum exactly to totalCost. Any accounting drift triggers a conservation error — the tool refuses to return an inconsistent quote.

Material cost and scrap yield

Raw material quotes rarely yield 100% good parts. Nesting loss, saw kerf, bar ends, casting risers, and first-off scrap inflate purchased weight. SC-012 uses a standard yield model:

effectiveMaterial = materialCost / (1 − scrapRate)

With scrapRate = 0.10, a €100 net material becomes €111.11 effective (10% loss). Enter scrap as a ratio (0.10), not percent (10). The guard scrapRate < 1 prevents divide-by-zero.

Non-uniform scrap (one expensive setup piece vs recurring runner loss) is not modeled per-part in v1.0.0. For highly asymmetric scrap, increase material cost or add a contingency line before margin.

💡
Pair material yield with process risk from SC-020 CNC Feeds & Speeds (tool breakage, roughing scrap) and SC-001 Weld Thickness (weld repair loops). Tighter tolerances from SC-008 often increase scrap and inspection labor — reflect that in scrap rate or overhead.

Labor, machine, and setup

Direct conversion cost is time × rate:

labor = laborHours × laborHourlyCost machine = machineHours × machineHourlyCost setup = (setupMinutes / 60) × setupHourlyCost

Labor hours should come from routings, CAM estimates, or time studies — not guesswork. Machine hours may track spindle time separately from labor (unattended machining). Setup is amortized over the quote quantity inside SC-012 only if you enter total setup minutes for the job; per-unit setup allocation is implicit when you quote batch quantity in the margin step.

Machine hourly burden typically includes depreciation, maintenance, power, and floor space. Do not embed the same fixed costs again in overhead unless your chart of accounts is designed for that split.

Shop overhead absorption

Overhead recovers indirect costs: rent, utilities, management, quality, IT, and shop supplies not tagged to the job.

overhead = directCost × overheadRate directCost = effMaterial + labor + machine + setup + energy + consumables + shipping

A 25% overhead rate on direct cost means €1,000 direct becomes €250 overhead. Activity-based costing (ABC) is not implemented — one flat rate assumes homogeneous job mix. Mixed low- and high-intensity jobs may need separate overhead rates or manual adjustment.

Payment terms and finance charge

When customers pay net 30, net 60, or longer, your working capital carries interest. SC-012 applies simple interest:

financeCharge = (directCost + overhead) × monthlyInterestRate × (paymentDays / 30)

This is a planning estimate, not compound interest or letter-of-credit fees. Increase monthlyInterestRate for risky accounts or currency exposure. Zero payment days removes finance from the stack.

Margin vs markup on sell price

SC-012 uses margin on sell price (also called gross margin percentage of price):

sellPrice = totalCost / (1 − targetMargin)

With total cost €800 and target margin 0.25 (25% of sell price), sell = €800 / 0.75 = €1,066.67. Profit = €266.67, which is exactly 25% of sell — not 25% of cost.

Markup on cost would be sell = cost × (1 + markup). A 25% markup on €800 gives €1,000 sell — lower than margin-on-price. Confusing the two is a classic quoting error; SC-012 enforces margin-on-price consistently. The guard targetMargin < 1 prevents invalid inputs.

markupOnCost equivalent: markup = targetMargin / (1 − targetMargin) (for margin 0.25 → markup = 0.333… on cost)

Sell price, unit price, and profit

unitPrice = sellPrice / quantity profit = sellPrice − totalCost profitPerUnit = profit / quantity

Quantity must be positive. For prototype qty 1, setup dominates unit price; for production qty 500, setup spreads thin. Run SC-012 twice when presenting both prototype and production tiers.

Risk contingencies

SC-012 has no dedicated contingency field — risk is absorbed by adjusting inputs:

  • Scrap rate — technical risk, new material, tight tolerances
  • Overhead rate — inspection, rework, engineering support
  • Labor hours — learning curve, manual deburr, secondary ops
  • Target margin — commercial risk, warranty, customer volatility
  • Finance charge — slow pay, export, bonding

Document which knob carries which risk so repricing stays auditable. Avoid stacking the same risk into scrap, overhead, and margin without labeling — that over-prices competitively sensitive jobs.

When to use SC-010 loaded labor rates

Always use true hourly cost from SC-010 True Labor Cost as SC-012's laborHourlyCost when labor is shop employees on payroll. SC-010 grosses net pay, adds employer SS/unemployment, benefits, severance accrual, overtime, and recruitment — the same dollars payroll actually spends.

Use cases:

  • Welding / fabrication — load SC-010 welder rate; estimate hours from weld length and WPS or use SC-001 for sizing context
  • Machining — operator labor at SC-010 rate; machine spindle at separate machine burden; cycle time from SC-020
  • Blended shop rate — weighted average of SC-010 outputs by headcount when routings are not detailed

Do not use SC-010 for subcontractor invoices quoted as lump sum — enter those as material or a direct line. Do not use net pay: you will under-recover roughly 30–80% depending on country preset.

Frequently asked questions

Does SC-012 use margin or markup?

Margin on sell price: sell = totalCost / (1 − targetMargin). A 30% target margin means 30% of the invoice is gross profit, not 30% added on top of cost. Markup-on-cost would use sell = cost × (1 + markup) and yields a lower sell price for the same nominal percentage.

How do I enter scrap — percent or ratio?

Enter scrap as a ratio between 0 and 1. Ten percent scrap is 0.10, not 10. The formula material / (1 − scrapRate) blows up as scrap approaches 100%; the engine rejects scrapRate ≥ 1.

Why doesn't SC-012 pull labor rate automatically from SC-010?

Tools stay independent in v1.0.0 so quotes remain reproducible and offline. Copy trueHourlyCost manually and note the date and preset in your quote worksheet. Automatic cross-tool linking may come in a later release.

Where do setup costs go for a quantity of 100?

Enter total setup minutes for the job and setup hourly rate; SC-012 adds full setup to job total cost, then divides sell price by quantity for unit price. Setup is not auto-spread — the quantity field only affects unit price after total sell is computed.

Is overhead double-counted if I use loaded labor from SC-010?

SC-010 loaded labor includes employer taxes and benefits, not shop rent or plant supervision. Overhead in SC-012 still applies unless your SC-010 inputs already embed plant burden — which would be non-standard. Keep employer burden in SC-010 and facility burden in SC-012 overhead.
