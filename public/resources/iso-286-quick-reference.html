ISO 286 Quick Reference

Use this page as a pocket reference when selecting ISO 286 fit classes during design reviews.

Hole-basis starting points

Fit symbolFit typeTypical use
H7/g6ClearanceSliding assembly, guide bushes, low-speed spindles.
H7/h6ClearancePush-fit parts that must slide or be repositioned by hand.
H7/k6TransitionLocation with light press; parts can be knocked down.
H7/n6TransitionLocation that needs to hold position without a locating key.
H7/p6InterferencePermanent location; verify stress and assembly force.

Shaft-basis equivalents

If your design fixes the shaft (h6) instead of the hole (H7), the same clearance/transition/interference families are available by letter: G7/h6 (clearance), K7/h6 (transition), P7/h6 (interference). Use the shaft-basis form when the shaft is a purchased bearing journal or a ground standard part.

Fundamental deviation and IT grade

The letter (H, g, k, p, ...) sets the fundamental deviation — the position of the tolerance zone relative to the nominal size. The number (IT5, IT6, IT7, ...) sets the magnitude of the zone. Lower IT grades give tighter zones: IT5 is roughly ±0.4 × 0.6√(D1/3) µm territory, IT7 about 16 times wider than IT5 for the same diameter.

Selection checklist

  • Pick hole-basis when the hole is machined in-house and the shaft is bought off-the-shelf.
  • Confirm assembly method — press-fit interference needs force or temperature analysis.
  • Check the actual operating temperature; thermal expansion can convert a clearance fit into an interference fit.
  • Verify material pair hardness for interference fits to avoid surface damage during assembly.
  • Always compute the worst-case and statistical gap range on the tolerance stack-up calculator before freezing the fit.
