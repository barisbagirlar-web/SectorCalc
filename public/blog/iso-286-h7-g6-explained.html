ISO 286 H7/g6 explained

Direct answer: H7 describes a hole tolerance zone relative to the nominal size; g6 describes a shaft zone. Together they define a clearance fit family — but the numbers that matter are the limit deviations at the actual nominal diameter, not the letters alone.

What engineers get wrong

“We always use H7/g6” without checking the diameter band is how assemblies that felt loose at Ø20 become tight (or vice versa) at Ø80. The grade letters are not a constant millimetre clearance.

Practical workflow

  1. Confirm the nominal size and whether the hole or shaft is the basic feature.
  2. Look up limit deviations for the selected zones in the applicable ISO 286 size range.
  3. If stack-up risk remains, move from fit selection to a 1D tolerance stack — that is a different tool.
