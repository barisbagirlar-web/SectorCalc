Bend allowance and bend deduction translate a 3D bend into a flat blank. Wrong K-factor or inside-radius assumptions miss the brake by millimetres — expensive when the nest is already cut. SectorCalc SC-030 computes allowance, deduction, and flat length from thickness, angle, inside radius, and K-factor — free, formula-visible. Catalog defaults start the conversation; your brake and material lot own production K-factors.

Why blank length fails first

If the flat is wrong, every downstream hit compounds the error. Brake setup cannot “absorb” a systematically short blank without flange growth fights. Screen blank length before nesting.

Inputs that actually matter

  • Thickness and material temper
  • Bend angle
  • Inside radius (tooling reality, not CAD wish)
  • K-factor (shop-proven when available)

Bend allowance vs bend deduction

Flat ≈ legs + bend allowance  |  Flat ≈ legs − bend deduction

Pick one accounting style for the nest and document it. Mixing BA and BD without a method note creates blank chaos across programmers.

K-factor calibration

Run a coupon on the production brake, measure developed length, and back-calculate an effective K-factor for that alloy/tooling pair. Store it where programmers can find it. Re-calibrate after material lot or tooling changes.

Worked pattern: first-hit screening

  1. Collect thickness, angle, inside radius, and starting K-factor.
  2. Run open SC-030.
  3. Review BA/BD and flat length; paste the K-factor basis on the traveler.
  4. Cut a coupon; compare measured vs predicted; update K-factor.
  5. If die work follows, open free SC-039.

Pair with punching force

Many sheet-metal jobs punch then bend. Keep both free calculators in the same traveler package under sheet-metal fabrication.

Common failure modes

  • One K-factor for every alloy
  • Ignoring inside radius growth
  • Mixing BA and BD methods
  • Skipping press capacity checks before die purchase

Neutral axis intuition

K-factor locates the neutral axis inside the thickness as a fraction from the inside surface. Soft materials and large radii push the neutral axis; tight radii and work-hardening alloys pull it. That is why a single catalog K destroys nests when the alloy or tooling changes.

Multi-bend flats

SC-030 screens a bend. Multi-bend parts need consistent accounting across every bend in the chain. Document order of bends when sequence changes developed length through prior strain. When in doubt, coupon the critical bend first, then expand.

CAD defaults vs shop reality

CAD bend tables are starting guesses. If your brake’s proven K-factor library disagrees with the CAD default, the shop library wins for released nests — and the disagreement belongs in the ECO notes so design stops re-injecting the wrong default.

Springback is separate

Allowance models material length in the bend. Springback compensation overbends the angle. Do not “fix” angle error by silently changing K-factor — that corrupts blank length while masking the real process control gap.

Additional FAQ

Air bend vs bottoming?

Tooling mode changes radius and force. Recalibrate K-factor when the process mode changes.

Imperial nests?

Keep units consistent. Do not mix mm thickness with inch flanges without conversion control.

Hemming?

Hems are special tooling cases — do not force a simple V-bend K-factor without coupon proof.

Related topic hub?

Sheet-metal fabrication.

Material tables vs coupons

Published K-factor tables are population averages. Your coil lot, grain direction, and tooling wear move the effective neutral axis. Coupon calibration is not optional for precision nests — it is how you stop arguing with the brake after every ECO.

When a customer locks a deduction method, follow it even if your internal K-factor library differs. Document the dual system: contract method for release, internal method for process learning.
