ISO 286 defines hole and shaft tolerance zones relative to a nominal size. Letter/number pairs like H7 or g6 are zone identities — not millimetre clearances. SectorCalc SC-027 looks up equation-backed families and computes limit deviations for the selected diameter band so you can screen a fit before drawings freeze. It does not invent unsupported zones and does not replace the governing ISO 286 edition on your contract.

What ISO 286 actually specifies

ISO 286 organises tolerances into a letter (fundamental deviation position relative to the zero line) and a number (IT grade — the size of the tolerance zone). The same letters mean different limit deviations in different nominal size ranges. Treating “H7/g6” as a constant clearance across 10 mm and 80 mm shafts is the most common drawing-room error.

Use ISO 286 when the functional question is local mating behaviour: sliding clearance, location transition, or light press. Use a 1D stack-up when the functional question is whether an assembly chain still closes.

Hole basis vs shaft basis

Most production systems are hole-basis: the hole stays on an H-system (fundamental deviation ≈ 0 for H), and the shaft grade (g, f, e, k, p…) sets clearance or transition behaviour. Shaft-basis systems exist; mixing systems without documenting which feature is basic creates ambiguous inspection plans.

Before you pick letters, write one sentence on the traveler: “Hole is basic (H7); shaft is g6 for easy sliding at Ø__.” That sentence prevents tribal fit folklore from overriding the print.

Limit deviations are size-dependent

SC-027 evaluates deviations at the actual nominal diameter you enter. Always re-check when the diameter band changes — even if the letter pair stays the same. Document the size range used for the lookup when you paste results into a drawing note or PPAP narrative.

Clearance, transition, interference

After both zones are known, extreme material conditions define the possible assembly states:

  • Clearance — minimum hole ≥ maximum shaft under the extremes you care about.
  • Transition — either clearance or light interference possible depending on actual sizes.
  • Interference — maximum hole ≤ minimum shaft (press/ shrink strategies).

State the intended class on the traveler. “H7/g6” without “easy sliding clearance” leaves assembly and QC arguing from memory.

Worked pattern: H7/g6 screening

  1. Confirm nominal diameter and whether the hole or shaft is basic.
  2. Open free SC-027 — no sign-in.
  3. Select the exposed hole/shaft family that matches the print language.
  4. Read ES/EI and es/ei for that size band; record min/max clearance or interference.
  5. If the assembly still has stacked contributors (housings, spacers, bearings), open credit-backed SC-008.

Honesty note: This pattern screens fit language. It does not certify press-fit assembly loads, thermal growth, or plating build-up unless you explicitly add those deltas.

When a fit is not enough — escalate to stack-up

Local fit success does not guarantee functional gap closure. Escalate when:

  • More than one feature controls the gap (bore depth + bearing width + spacer).
  • Customer asks for worst-case / RSS / Monte Carlo evidence.
  • Thermal or coating deltas are significant relative to the clearance band.

Unsupported zones and honesty rules

SC-027 exposes equation-backed families only. If a letter pair is outside the implemented set, the calculator must not invent a table. Keep the governing ISO 286 edition for release; use SectorCalc for screening and teaching the size-dependence of deviations.

Common failure modes

  • Copying H7/g6 from a previous job without re-looking up the diameter band.
  • Ignoring plating/temperature growth that consumes clearance.
  • Calling a transition fit “press” in the assembly instruction.
  • Using fit selection to skip stack-up on multi-contributor gaps.

Frequently asked questions

Is SC-027 free?

Yes. No sign-in, no credits. Instant screening results.

Does this replace ISO 286?

No. Keep the governing edition for contractual release. SC-027 screens exposed families with visible math.

Can I put SC-027 numbers on PPAP?

As engineering screening evidence only. Customer acceptance still governs.

Why did clearance change when I only changed diameter?

Because IT grades and fundamental deviations are tabulated by size range — same letters, different micrometres.

When do I open stack-up?

When multiple contributors — not a single hole/shaft pair — control the functional dimension.

Are all ISO letters supported?

Only equation-backed families exposed in the UI. Unsupported zones are not invented.

Hole-basis or shaft-basis?

Document which feature is basic. Most shops are hole-basis with H-system holes.
