Punching force estimates the press load needed to shear a perimeter through thickness at a given shear strength. Guessing tonnage from habit breaks punches and rings frames. SectorCalc SC-039 screens force from perimeter, thickness, and shear strength with explicit units — free. Manufacturer capacity charts and required safety factors still govern final selection.

Why guessed tonnage fails

Habit-based tonnage ignores perimeter growth on complex punches and material state (annealed vs hard). Downtime shows up as broken tooling, not just scrap.

Force estimate and units

F ≈ perimeter × thickness × shear strength

Handle units explicitly (N·mm vs tonf·in). Apply the safety factors required by your press standard before release.

Perimeter geometry matters

Slots, windows, and irregular contours are not “same as a round hole of equal area.” Effective shear length drives force. Measure or CAD-extract the true perimeter.

Capacity screening

Compare estimated force to press and tooling ratings. Near the limit, redesign the hit sequence or tool before steel is ordered. OEM charts win for purchase decisions.

Die clearance and edge quality

Force and edge quality both depend on clearance practice. Document clearance intent beside the force result so tooling and QC share one story.

Worked pattern: before tooling buy

  1. Extract perimeter and thickness; confirm shear strength for material state.
  2. Run open SC-039.
  3. Compare F to press/tool ratings with required safety factor.
  4. Document clearance practice and material state on the traveler.
  5. If forming follows, open free SC-030.

Common failure modes

  • Ignoring complex perimeter
  • Wrong shear strength basis
  • No clearance plan
  • Skipping OEM tonnage charts

Shear strength discipline

Shear strength is not ultimate tensile strength and not “whatever the last job used.” Use the value for the material state on the router (annealed, cold-worked, stainless condition). Wrong shear strength is the quietest way to under-size a press.

Hit sequence and stripper loads

A single-hit force screen is necessary but not always sufficient. Progressive dies and multi-hit patterns change peak demand and stripper requirements. When the estimate is near capacity, involve tooling engineering before steel is ordered — do not “hope the frame is fine.”

Force vs energy

Some presses are energy-limited at speed even when peak force looks acceptable. SC-039 screens force. Confirm energy and stroke with OEM data for high-speed or thick work.

Edge quality and secondary ops

Die clearance changes breakout and rollover. If the print demands a machined edge after punch, say so on the traveler so force screening and finishing plans stay aligned.

Traveler package

  • Perimeter source (CAD / measured)
  • Thickness + material state
  • Shear strength source
  • Estimated F + safety factor basis
  • Press/tool rating comparison
  • Clearance intent
  • Link to SC-030 when forming follows

Additional FAQ

What about shear factor < 1.0?

Some tooling practices apply a shear factor for angled punches. If you use one, document it — do not hide it inside an unexplained force number.

Stainless vs mild steel?

Do not reuse mild-steel habit numbers. Update shear strength and expect higher force.

Can I size the press from SC-039 alone?

No. Use it to screen, then confirm with OEM capacity and your safety policy.

Related guides?

Bend and CNC when machining follows.

Tool steel and punch life

Force screening prevents frame overload; it does not pick punch steel. When estimated force is high, expect heat and wear — involve tooling materials early. A “cheap” punch that snaps mid-lot costs more than the OEM chart review you skipped.
